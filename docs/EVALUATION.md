## What this measures, and what it cannot

- Relevance judgments come from declared synthetic personas, not from real users.
- Click-through and conversion are omitted: they require live traffic and cannot be measured here.
- Rating-error metrics (RMSE, MAE, MSE) do not apply; this system ranks and never predicts a rating.
- Baseline is the identical scorer with an empty profile, which is what a first-time visitor gets.

Coverage has a ceiling of 20.0% here: 6 personas times a cut-off of 10 over a
catalogue of 300. The personalized figure reaches 96.7% of that ceiling, meaning
the personas are being served almost entirely distinct inventory rather than
converging on the same few listings.

Category diversity is deliberately **lower** when personalized, and that is
the correct outcome rather than a regression. A visitor with a declared
preference should see a page concentrated in what they want; the baseline
looks more varied only because it is ignoring the visitor. This is the usual
accuracy against diversity trade-off, and it is worth watching: if a future
change pushed diversity toward zero it would mean the ranker had stopped
showing anything but one category, which is a filter bubble rather than a
recommendation.

## Method

Six personas are declared in `internal/ranking/eval.go`, each stating a
category, a price band, wanted amenities and a vibe. Relevance is graded 0 to
3 by a rule printed in that file. Each persona's profile is fed to the same
`Rank` function the service uses in production; the baseline is that identical
function with an empty profile, which is what a first-time visitor receives.

The harness is exercised by `go test ./internal/ranking/`, which fails if
personalization ever stops beating the baseline.
